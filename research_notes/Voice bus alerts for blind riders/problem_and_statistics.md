> Method note: Direct page fetching was blocked in this research session (network policy: ipn.md, statistica.gov.md, media.rnib.org.uk did not resolve). ## 1. Global and European numbers of blind and visually impaired people

### Takeaway
About 43 million people worldwide are blind and about 295 million have moderate-to-severe vision impairment (2020, Lancet GBD/VLEG). Blindness is projected to reach about 61 million by 2050. WHO's broader figure is 2.2 billion with some vision impairment. Europe-wide, the European Blind Union's long-standing estimate is over 30 million blind and partially sighted people, but the EBU itself is reviewing that number.

### Cited Findings
- 2020: an estimated 43.3 million people blind (95% UI 37.6–48.4), 295 million with moderate/severe vision impairment (MSVI), and 258 million with mild vision impairment. GBD 2019 Blindness and Vision Impairment Collaborators / VLEG, *Lancet Global Health* 2021;9:e130–e143 — [PubMed 33275950](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33275950/); [UCL repository PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10120700/1/1-s2.0-S2214109X20304253-main.pdf)
- From 1990 to 2020 the number of blind people rose 50.6% and the number with MSVI rose 91.7%. Over the same period, age-standardised blindness prevalence among people aged 50+ fell 28.5%. The rise in counts is attributed to population growth and ageing — [PubMed 33275950](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33275950/)
- IAPB Vision Atlas: about 1.1 billion people had some vision loss in 2020 (43M blind, 295M MSVI, 258M mild, 510M near-vision loss). Projected for 2050: 61M blind and 474M MSVI — [IAPB Vision Atlas Evidence Series: Magnitude of Vision Loss (2021)](https://www.iapb.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Vision-Atlas_Evidence-Series_Magnitude-of-VIsion-Loss_24022021.pdf); [Statista chart based on IAPB](https://statista.com/chart/31502/expected-number-of-people-with-vision-loss-globally); [Vision Atlas](https://visionatlas.iapb.org)
- Earlier VLEG baseline (2015, older data): 36M blind and 217M MSVI — [Community Eye Health Journal / PMC5820628](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5820628/)
- WHO *World Report on Vision* (Oct 2019): at least 2.2 billion people have a vision impairment, of whom at least 1 billion have an impairment that could have been prevented or has not been addressed. Leading causes include uncorrected refractive error (124M) and cataract (65M) — [Sightsavers summary of WHO report](https://www.sightsavers.org/news/2019/10/more-than-a-billion-people-have-avoidable-vision-impairment); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/10/8/who-1-billion-people-suffer-from-preventable-eye-conditions)
- Europe: the EBU estimates "over 30 million blind and partially sighted persons in geographical Europe". It says partially sighted people outnumber blind people 4 to 1, and nearly 90% are over 60 — [EBU facts and figures](https://euroblind.org/about-blindness-and-partial-sight/facts-and-figures)
- The EBU Board (Oct 2025 newsletter) wants to check whether the 30 million estimate still reflects the latest data. An earlier figure of 26.35 million was cited in a 2014 European Parliament question — [EBU newsletter Oct 2025](https://www.euroblind.org/newsletter/2025/october/en/statistics-number-visually-impaired-people-europe); [European Parliament E-004556/2014](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/E-7-2014-004556_EN.pdf)
- A Statista dataset gives the 2020 number of people with vision loss in Eastern Europe by cause and severity (I did not get the values) — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1201022/vision-impairments-in-eastern-europe-by-cause/)

### Inferences
- About 338M people (43M blind + 295M MSVI) would find it hard or impossible to read a bus route number on an approaching vehicle. This is the core global addressable population for audio bus identification.
- Ageing drives the growth. Because ~90% of visually impaired Europeans are over 60, the user base overlaps with older riders, who also tend to depend more on buses.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025/2026 update of the VLEG/IAPB figures. The 2020 estimates appear to be the latest.
- I could not get a breakdown of IAPB 2020 counts for the Central/Eastern Europe region. Statista has the data, but the numbers were not visible to me.

## 2. Republic of Moldova and Romania: affected populations; Chișinău context

### Takeaway
Moldova has no clean, current count of blind people. In the 2024 Census, about 30% of residents (over 560,000 people) reported some vision difficulty even with glasses. About 6% of those reported severe impairment or total blindness, which works out to roughly 30,000+ people (my estimate). Older figures put it at 3,000+ blind and 9,000 sight-impaired, and the Society of the Blind reported about 9,500 members. Moldova has about 160–176 thousand people with a recognised disability. Romania has about 981,000 people with a disability certificate (end 2025), but I found no visual-only breakdown. Chișinău municipality has about 720,000 residents.

### Cited Findings — Moldova
- 2024 Population and Housing Census (8 Apr–7 Jul 2024): almost 30% of citizens reported vision difficulties even with glasses or contact lenses. Of these, 6% have severe vision impairment or total blindness — [IPN: "Every third Moldovan faces vision difficulties"](https://ipn.md/en/every-third-moldovan-faces-vision-difficulties-7967_1111425.html)
- Over 560,000 people experience some form of vision difficulty (2024 Census) — [Radio Moldova](https://radiomoldova.md/p/78916)
- The NBS census release on daily-activity difficulties (RPL2024) is the primary source. Its numbers were not visible in the extract — [BNS: RPL2024 dificultățile populației în realizarea activităților zilnice](https://statistica.gov.md/ro/print/rpl2024-dificultatile-populatiei-in-realizarea-activitatilor-zilnice--10124_62065.html)
- Older figure, undated (likely pre-2019): "over 3,000 blind and 9,000 sight-impaired persons, including about 400 children" — [IPN (old)](https://www.old.ipn.md/en/over-3000-blind-and-9000-sight-impaired-persons-live-in-moldova-7967_978052.html)

### Inferences
- From the 2024 census, 6% of ~560,000 is roughly 33,000 people in Moldova reporting severe vision impairment or blindness. This self-reported figure is much higher than the ~3,000 registered blind. ## 3. Evidence that bus identification and boarding are documented pain points for blind/low-vision riders

### Takeaway
UK charity surveys from 2011 to 2025 repeatedly show that blind and partially sighted people cannot reliably identify or board the right bus. Drivers announce the route number only about half the time when asked. Most riders have missed stops. In 2025, fewer than half (Wales: 1 in 10) could make all the bus journeys they need. Academic work confirms that blind riders "are not able to see buses coming, or identify the route number or destination".

### Cited Findings
- RNIB *All Aboard? Navigating bus travel with sight loss* (online survey Feb 2025, n=1,197, plus focus groups): fewer than half of blind and partially sighted people can make all the bus journeys they want or need. 83% say having the option to travel by bus is "very important". 49% of those who met inaccessible bus-stop bypasses make fewer journeys — [RNIB news](https://www.rnib.org.uk/news/all-aboard-our-new-report-on-navigating-bus-travel-with-sight-loss/); [RNIB report page](https://www.rnib.org.uk/professionals/research-and-data/reports-and-insight/all-aboard-navigating-bus-travel-with-sight-loss/); [AOP coverage, May 2025](https://www.aop.org.uk/ot/news/2025/05/21/inaccessible-bus-travel-leaving-blind-and-partially-sighted-people-trapped-at-home-says-rnib)
- The exact figure is 46%, and 80% find unfamiliar bus journeys difficult or very difficult (secondary blog; I did not verify this against RNIB) — [Vix Technology blog](https://blog.vixtechnology.com/blog/designing-bus-journeys-for-everyone-tackling-sight-loss-barriers-in-public-transport)
- RNIB Cymru *All Aboard?* (2025; survey of 146 people in Wales, 2024): only 1 in 10 can make all the bus journeys they want or need. 51% have difficulty finding the correct bus stop on familiar journeys and 61% on unfamiliar ones. Drivers often lack awareness of how to support passengers with sight loss — [RNIB Wales news](https://www.rnib.org.uk/news/bus-services-are-failing-their-blind-and-partially-sighted-passengers-in-wales/); [RNIB Cymru report PDF](https://media.rnib.org.uk/documents/RNIB_Cymru_-_All_Aboard_-_Bus_Services_report_2025.pdf)
- Guide Dogs *Road to Nowhere* (2013; older data): drivers announce the bus number only about half the time when asked. The search extracts give two figures, "50% of drivers always communicate this information" and "only 55% always say the number of the bus they are driving when asked", so check the PDF — [Road to Nowhere PDF (WCB)](https://wcb-ccd.org.uk/library/road_to_nowhere_report_22_04_2013.pdf)
- Guide Dogs *Forgotten Passengers* (2011–2012; older): 87% of passengers with sight loss had missed their stop at some point because they did not know where they were on the bus — [CBW Magazine](https://cbwmagazine.com/guide-dogs-survey-reveals-forgotten-passengers/amp/)
- Guide Dogs *Destination Unknown* (2014, >2,000 respondents; older): 65% of blind and partially sighted bus passengers missed their stop at least once in the last six months. The campaign called for audio-visual ("talking bus") announcements on all new buses — [Guide Dogs Destination Unknown PDF](https://guidedogs.org.uk/-/media/Project/GuideDogs/GuideDogsDotOrg/Files/How-you-can-help/talking-buses-campaign/1252_7603-a4-destination-unknown_v8-1.pdf); [PoliticsHome](https://www.politicshome.com/news/article/too-many-people-heading-for-a-destination-unknown); [Talking Buses toolkit](https://guidedogs.org.uk/-/media/Project/GuideDogs/GuideDogsDotOrg/Files/How-you-can-help/talking-buses-campaign/12522_talking-buses-toolkit_accessible.pdf)
- Academic research: a UC Berkeley report notes that blind riders "are not able to see buses coming, or to identify the route number or destination displayed on those buses" — [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt79n6s0p0/qt79n6s0p0.pdf?t=krnjqa). Azenkot et al. (CHI 2011, University of Washington) studied blind riders' bus-stop and arrival information needs — [azenkot-chi2011 PDF](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~borning/papers/azenkot-chi2011.pdf)
- Mass Eye and Ear *All_Aboard* app study: blind users found bus stops about 91–93% of the time with the app, against 52% with Google Maps (sources differ slightly). This shows that stop-finding without assistive tech fails about half the time — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1032773); [AOP Feb 2024](https://www.aop.org.uk/ot/news/2024/02/03/technology-helps-commuters-with-sight-loss-identify-bus-stops)
- American Foundation for the Blind keeps a transportation fact sheet on travel barriers for people with vision loss (I did not extract figures) — [AFB](https://afb.org/research-and-initiatives/research/fact-sheets/transportation)
- Moldova (2026 draft regulation): road-transport infrastructure is not uniformly adapted for persons with reduced mobility — [particip.gov.md](https://particip.gov.md/ro/download_attachment/37682)

### Inferences
- Even in the UK, which has mandatory accessible-information rules, about half of drivers do not reliably state the bus number when asked. In a city with no audio external announcements, such as Chișinău (see section 4), blind riders likely depend entirely on asking strangers or the driver.
- The repeated "missed stop" figures (65–87%) concern on-board location, which is a separate problem from identifying the arriving bus. Both point to the same missing piece: real-time audio information.

### Gaps
- I found no survey giving the % of blind riders who "cannot identify the arriving bus number", or a % for "drivers not stopping when hailed". The RNIB 2025 full reports may contain these but could not be fetched.
- I found no Moldovan or Romanian survey of blind people's public-transport barriers.
- I found no EU-level (EBU or Commission) quantitative survey specific to bus identification.

## 4. Legal context and Chișinău's current audio announcement provision

### Takeaway
Moldova ratified the UN CRPD in 2010 (Law 166/2010), and Law 60/2012 governs the social inclusion of persons with disabilities. I did not see any concrete requirement for external audio announcements in Chișinău. Romania's ANPC Order 579/2024 does require public transport operators to install external/internal audio signals announcing stops. The European Accessibility Act (in force 28 June 2025) excludes urban/suburban transport from its real-time travel information obligations, though urban operators remain covered for terminals and ticketing. In Chișinău, articulated trolleybuses were reported to be getting automatic audio stop announcements. I found no evidence of external route-number announcements at stops.

### Cited Findings
- Moldova ratified the CRPD in 2010: Law No. 166 of 9 July 2010, international deposit 21 Sep 2010 (signed 30 Mar 2007) — [UNPRPD Moldova](https://mptf.undp.org/sites/default/files/documents/15000/unprpd_moldova.pdf); [UN Geneva CRPD Committee 2017](https://ungeneva.org/en/news-media/meeting-summary/2017/03/committee-rights-persons-disabilities-considers-initial-report); [Ombudsman shadow report on CRPD 2024](https://ombudsman.md/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/shadow-report-on-un-convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities-2024.pdf)
- Law No. 60 of 30.03.2012 on the social inclusion of persons with disabilities (Moldova). The text is available, but I did not extract the transport/information articles — [Law 60/2012 PDF (kdu.md mirror)](https://kdu.md/images/Files/admitere2026/ro-lege-rm-privind-incluziunea-sociala-a-persoanelor-cu-dizabilitati-nr60-2012.pdf); [USM mirror](https://usm.md/wp-content/uploads/Anexa-3.4.13.-Legea-privind-incluziunea-sociala-a-pers_cu-dizabilitati-1.pdf)
- Romania: ANPC Order no. 579/2024 (4 June 2024; Monitorul Oficial no. 547 of 12 June 2024) requires public transport operators to inform consumers with disabilities and to install external/internal audio signals announcing stops. Operators must also set up dedicated information methods for people with visual impairments. It applies to road, rail and naval operators — [ANPC draft order PDF](https://anpc.ro/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ordin-informare-persoane-cu-dizabilitati-oct-cu-obs-Consiliu-site-si-informare.pdf); [alba24.ro](https://alba24.ro/firmele-de-transport-public-obligate-sa-afiseze-informatii-utile-persoanelor-cu-dizabilitati-proiect-de-ordin-la-anpc-995656.html); [Antena3](https://www.antena3.ro/actualitate/anpc-ordin-obligatii-aeroport-operatori-transport-sprijin-persoane-dizabilitati-712823.html). The general Romanian framework is Law 448/2006.
- European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882): transport service obligations apply from 28 June 2025. Annex I applies real-time travel information obligations to air, bus, rail and waterborne services "except for urban and suburban transport services and regional transport services". Those services remain covered for self-service terminals and ticketing — [ACI Europe annex reproduction](https://aci-europe.org/component/attachments/attachments.html?id=3452); [Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/eaa-transportation-services/); [European Commission, 31 Jul 2025](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/eu-becomes-more-accessible-all-2025-07-31_en?prefLang=de)
- Chișinău: IPN reported that new articulated trolleybuses have modern information systems and that the municipality was preparing automatic audio announcements of arrival stations (on-board; undated) — [IPN: State-of-the-art information systems in articulated trolleybuses](https://ipn.md/en/state-of-the-art-information-systems-in-articulated-trolleybuses-in-7967_1102415.html)
- Chișinău: more than 20 second-hand trolleybuses from Estonia and Latvia (2025) have ramps and low floors. No audio announcements are mentioned — [EVZ](https://evz.ro/peste-20-de-troleibuze-moderne-din-estonia-si-letonia-vor-circula-pe-strazile-din-chisinau.html)
- A municipal procurement for trolleybus information materials specifies black-and-white contrast for readability by visually impaired people (visual, not audio) — [MTender](https://storage.mtender.gov.md/get/ba716b09-768a-41b6-a503-8c03e3351682-1785223627155)
- Chișinău has installed sound devices at pedestrian crossings (traffic lights), but not at bus stops — [IPN traffic-light tag](https://ipn.md/en/tag/traffic-light/)
- Municipal transport quality standards note — [chisinau.md: Not informativ standarde transport](https://new.chisinau.md/files/Not_informativ_standarde_transport.pdf) (contents not extracted)

### Inferences
- Chișinău's audio provision, where it exists, seems limited to on-board next-stop announcements on newer trolleybuses. I found no source mentioning external speakers announcing the route number to people waiting, or audio displays at stops. That matches the gap a voice-alert app would fill.
- The EAA's urban exclusion means EU law will not force cities to provide accessible real-time arrival info. Moldova is an EU candidate, so EAA alignment matters, but city-level gaps would likely persist. Romania's ANPC order is a closer regional precedent.

### Gaps
- I did not check the specific articles of Law 60/2012 on transport accessibility and information.
- I found no confirmation of whether Chișinău's on-board audio announcements were actually rolled out, or on what share of the fleet (RTEC or Parcul Urban de Autobuze).
- I found no reporting from point.md, newsmaker.md or agora.md on blind people's experiences with Chișinău buses, possibly because of search limits.
- The ANPC Order 579/2024 is final (published in Monitorul Oficial), but I have not verified that its audio requirement is unchanged or how far it has been implemented.
