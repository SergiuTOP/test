# Build the voice layer, not the tracker

**Building this is realistic, and the useful version is a narrow app, not a broad one.** Chișinău already streams GPS positions for most municipal trolleybuses and buses. A community MQTT feed sends route number, fleet number, heading and speed about every 3 seconds, and the Romanian firm Tranzy runs a token-based API covering the city's two operators, RTEC and PUA. What does not exist in Chișinău is a voice-first, Romanian/Russian app that sits at a stop and says "trolleybus 22 arriving in about 40 seconds, then 3." That is a 3–5 month job for a small team (researchers' estimate), and the hard parts are not technical. They are getting a durable data agreement with the city operators, covering the private minibuses (*rutiere*), which appear to have no tracking, and being honest that GPS alone cannot say *which* vehicle is at the door when two pull in together. The need is real: Moldova's 2024 census implies roughly **33,000 people with severe vision impairment or blindness** (estimate from census percentages), and UK surveys show blind riders cannot reliably identify buses even where drivers are required to help. My recommendation: build a free GPS-feed app with voice and haptic output as the MVP, co-design it with the Asociația Nevăzătorilor din Moldova, and treat on-vehicle Bluetooth beacons (about €7–10k of hardware for the fleet, estimate) as phase two, following the precedent Bucharest set in 2015 and again in 2025. Do not lead with camera recognition. It works in papers and asks too much of a blind user standing at a curb.

## Roughly 33,000 Moldovans cannot read a route number

The global numbers set the scale. In 2020 an estimated **43.3 million people were blind and 295 million had moderate-to-severe vision impairment** ([Lancet Global Health / PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33275950/)) (snippet). Blindness is projected to reach **61 million by 2050** ([IAPB Vision Atlas](https://www.iapb.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Vision-Atlas_Evidence-Series_Magnitude-of-VIsion-Loss_24022021.pdf)) (snippet). Ageing drives the growth: the European Blind Union says partially sighted people outnumber blind people 4 to 1 and that **nearly 90% are over 60** ([EBU](https://euroblind.org/about-blindness-and-partial-sight/facts-and-figures)) (snippet). Your friend's example user, someone with 2% vision, falls in the blind or severe category. Partial sight helps that person in some situations, but it will not let them read an LED route sign on a moving vehicle.

Moldova has no clean registry count, so you have to work from a range. In the 2024 census almost **30% of residents reported some vision difficulty even with glasses, and 6% of those reported severe impairment or total blindness** ([IPN](https://ipn.md/en/every-third-moldovan-faces-vision-difficulties-7967_1111425.html)) (snippet). Radio Moldova put the total with any vision difficulty at **over 560,000** ([Radio Moldova](https://radiomoldova.md/p/78916)) (snippet). Six percent of 560,000 is **about 33,000 people** (estimate). That figure is self-reported and functional. The registered numbers are far lower: an older IPN item cites **over 3,000 blind and 9,000 sight-impaired people** ([IPN, undated](https://www.old.ipn.md/en/over-3000-blind-and-9000-sight-impaired-persons-live-in-moldova-7967_978052.html)) (snippet), and the Society of the Blind reported **over 9,500 members, fewer than 2,000 of them employed** ([IPN](https://ipn.md/en/factory-bankruptcies-leave-visually-impaired-persons-unemployed-7967_1000890.html)) (snippet). Chișinău municipality had **720,100 residents in 2024, 29.9% of the national population** ([BNS](https://statistica.gov.md/en/final-results-of-the-2024-population-and-housing-census-geographical-distributio-10121_61877.html)) (snippet). Scaling by population gives **roughly 3,500 to 10,000 people in the city** with severe impairment (estimate), from the registered floor up to the census ceiling. In practice the realistic user base is the few hundred to few thousand of them who own a smartphone and ride regularly (estimate). That is enough to matter to a city, and too few to support a commercial app on its own.

There is no Moldovan survey of blind people's transit barriers, so the evidence that bus identification hurts comes from the UK. RNIB's 2025 survey of **1,197 people** found that **fewer than half of blind and partially sighted people can make all the bus journeys they need**, and 83% call bus travel "very important" ([RNIB](https://www.rnib.org.uk/news/all-aboard-our-new-report-on-navigating-bus-travel-with-sight-loss/)) (snippet). In Wales, where 146 people were surveyed, the figure was **only 1 in 10** ([RNIB Cymru](https://www.rnib.org.uk/news/bus-services-are-failing-their-blind-and-partially-sighted-passengers-in-wales/)) (snippet). Guide Dogs found that drivers **announce the route number only about half the time when asked** ([Road to Nowhere, 2013](https://wcb-ccd.org.uk/library/road_to_nowhere_report_22_04_2013.pdf)) (snippet; the extracts give both 50% and 55%), and that **65% of blind passengers had missed their stop in the previous six months** ([Destination Unknown, 2014](https://guidedogs.org.uk/-/media/Project/GuideDogs/GuideDogsDotOrg/Files/How-you-can-help/talking-buses-campaign/1252_7603-a4-destination-unknown_v8-1.pdf)) (snippet). UC Berkeley researchers put the core problem plainly: blind riders "are not able to see buses coming, or to identify the route number or destination" ([eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt79n6s0p0/qt79n6s0p0.pdf?t=krnjqa)). The UK has mandatory accessible-information rules and Chișinău does not. The only audio provision the researchers found in Chișinău is planned on-board stop announcements on new articulated trolleybuses ([IPN](https://ipn.md/en/state-of-the-art-information-systems-in-articulated-trolleybuses-in-7967_1102415.html)) (snippet). Nobody announces route numbers to people waiting at the stop.

The law will not close this gap any time soon. Moldova ratified the UN disability convention in 2010 ([UNPRPD](https://mptf.undp.org/sites/default/files/documents/15000/unprpd_moldova.pdf)). The European Accessibility Act, in force since June 2025, **specifically exempts urban and suburban transport from its real-time travel information duties** ([Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/eaa-transportation-services/)). Romania's ANPC Order 579/2024 goes further and requires operators to install external and internal audio stop signals ([ANPC](https://anpc.ro/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/ordin-informare-persoane-cu-dizabilitati-oct-cu-obs-Consiliu-site-si-informare.pdf)) (snippet). That gives Moldova, as an EU candidate, a nearby precedent to point to, but it creates no obligation there.

## Chișinău already streams the data, every three seconds

This is the main reason the idea is realistic. The community project **Roataway** documents a public MQTT broker at `opendata.dekart.com` (port 1945, and 1946 for websockets). Vehicles send telemetry **about every 3 seconds**, with latitude, longitude, heading, speed and timestamp, and the per-route topics add the **route number and fleet board number** ([roataway/api-documentation](https://github.com/roataway/api-documentation)). The same project publishes routes, per-route stations and segments, and a vehicle list with accessibility flags ([infrastructure-data](https://github.com/roataway/infrastructure-data)), plus a community static GTFS ([gtfs-data](https://github.com/roataway/gtfs-data)). Dekart appears to be the vendor of the trackers themselves and is credited with "opening it up to the public". No licence is stated.

The second source is commercial. **Tranzy.ai**, a Romanian company, runs an OpenData API that "partially implements" GTFS, is labelled beta, needs a token for each agency, and lists **"RTEC&PUA Chisinau"** as an agency ([horace42/tranzy-stats](https://github.com/horace42/tranzy-stats); [Tranzy opendata](https://tranzy.ai/opendata/)) (snippet). Tranzy claims **more than 150,000 daily users** across Iași, Cluj, Botoșani and Chișinău, and in 2023 it described an audio feature for visually impaired users as "soon to be launched". The researchers found no evidence that it ever launched ([Business Review](https://issuu.com/businessreview/docs/br_04_2023/s/24634366)) (snippet). On the city side, the mobility platform went live on 15 March 2023 with tracking on **more than 400 trolleybuses and 210 buses** ([Moldpres](https://www.moldpres.md/en/news/2023/03/15/23002070)) (snippet). RTEC's tender documents require trackers to stream JSON to an endpoint that RTEC provides ([MTender](https://storage.mtender.gov.md/get/99b21cf5-34a2-44d5-af2f-40493f314d11-1639164725396)) (snippet), so RTEC is the real owner of the data. The scale is meaningful: buses and minibuses alone carried **75.7 million passengers in 2024, up 39% on 2023** ([BNS Chișinău bulletin](https://statistica.gov.md/files/files/publicatii_electronice/Chisinau/trimestrial/Chisinau_anul_2024.pdf)) (snippet), and that count excludes trolleybuses.

The feed has four weaknesses that will shape the product:

- **Not every vehicle has a tracker.** The Roataway data says so explicitly, and Tranzy's App Store reviews complain about the same thing ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/md/app/tranzy/id1492365417)).
- **No source shows that the private *rutiere* are tracked.** An app that stays silent about a minibus is safe only if it tells the user that minibuses are not covered.
- **Direction of travel has to be inferred.** The payload gives a compass heading but no trip ID or direction ID, so the app must match each vehicle to route segments to know which terminus it is heading toward.
- **The contracts change hands.** One GPS-tracking contract expired in July 2022 ([IPN](https://ipn.md/en/platform-enabling-to-monitor-each-trolleybus-in-chisinau-in-real-7967_1089295.html)) (snippet), and another ran to December 2025 ([achizitii.md](https://achizitii.md/en/public/tender/21311663/lot/11934592)) (snippet).

The researchers could not connect to the broker, so **nobody has confirmed that the Dekart feed is still live in November 2026**. Testing it is the first afternoon of work on this project.

## Every existing app stops 50 metres short

Mainstream apps give arrival times, not identification. **Moovit** is the most accessible of them. It has supported VoiceOver and TalkBack since 2016, gives condensed spoken directions and get-off alerts ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/03/30/moovit-expands-accessibility-features-for-blind-users)), and feeds the WeWALK smart cane ([Moovit](https://moovit.com/press-releases/moovit-and-wewalk-announce-partnership-to-enhance-mobility-of-visually-impaired-people/)). Moldova's tourism site recommends Moovit for Chișinău ([moldova.travel](https://booking.moldova.travel/help)), but no source confirms that it carries live rather than scheduled data there. The research found no Chișinău app, Tranzy included, that will alert a blind user by voice when a chosen route is about to reach their stop.

Blind-specific tools are strong, but none has been deployed in Moldova. **NaviLens** codes can be read **from about 14–30+ metres without aiming**, according to the vendor ([NaviLens](https://www.navilens.com/en/sectors/transport/buses)) (snippet). Madrid's EMT tagged **all 5,540 stops** ([NaviLens EMT](https://www.navilens.com/en/case-studies/emt-madrid)) (snippet), and Barcelona logs **about 40,000 reads a month** ([Catalan News](https://www.catalannews.com/society-science/item/navilens-system-revolutionizes-public-transport-for-visually-impaired-with-40000-reads-monthly)) (snippet). NaviLens tells you about arrivals *at the stop*, though, not which vehicle is pulling in, and it needs an agency contract. General AI vision apps such as Be My AI, Seeing AI and Lookout can read text, but a 2020 review called them "not tailored to use in identification of bus numbers, especially at a large distance" ([Tang](https://brandontang89.github.io/static_files/reports/bus.pdf)).

The closest match to your friend's idea is next door in Romania. In 2015 the Cluj firm Onyx Beacon fitted **500 iBeacons to Bucharest buses and trolleybuses**. A user picks a line, and the phone announces it when that vehicle comes within about **50–70 metres** ([RFID Journal](https://www.rfidjournal.com/?p=72206); [Playtech](https://playtech.ro/2015/clujenii-ofera-solutia-de-siguranta-pentru-nevazatori-in-transportul-public/)) (snippet; the sources give different ranges). Sibiu put beacons on **all 160 buses and 840 stops** ([RFID Journal](https://www.rfidjournal.com/?p=72250)) (snippet). In 2025 Bucharest's STB launched **STEP HEAR** on lines 90 and 104. It gives audio guidance to the stop and vehicle-arrival announcements, warns the driver in advance, and was co-developed with the Asociația Nevăzătorilor din România, with about **6,000 expected beneficiaries** ([Romania Insider](https://www.romania-insider.com/bucharest-public-transport-app-visually-impaired-2025)) (snippet). Warsaw's 2014 beacon pilot cost about €1M ([OECD OPSI](https://oecd-opsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Virtual-Warsaw_Poland_2014.pdf)). It and the 2015 Bucharest system both seem to have faded, which suggests that hardware projects die when nobody owns the maintenance. Two good ideas are worth copying from elsewhere: Korea's Gyeonggi "bus stop bell", which sounds an alert at the driver's seat when a rider within 30 m requests the bus ([Gyeonggi](https://english.gg.go.kr/?p=17046)), and the US ADA rule requiring **external audible route announcements at boarding areas** ([eCFR 1192.61](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-36/chapter-XI/part-1192/subpart-C/section-1192.61)).

## GPS can say "22 in 40 seconds" but not "this one, at the door"

Physics sets the product's limits. The vehicle feed is fast, but the user's phone is not precise. In DXOMARK tests the best phones averaged **under 5 m horizontal error and weaker ones about 8 m** ([DXOMARK](https://www.dxomark.com/gps-on-your-smartphone-why-youre-not-always-there-when-it-says-youre-there/)). Older downtown tests of mass-market phones showed **95% error circles of 50–100 m** ([Fierce Network](https://www.fierce-network.com/europe/navigate-city)), and a 2026 study finds that the environment, not the handset, drives most of the error ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26706v1)). Add the 3-second telemetry interval, the broker and the network, and the total delay is roughly **5–10 seconds, in which a vehicle at 20–30 km/h covers 30–80 m** (estimate). Two consequences follow. First, alerts have to be predictive, firing when the vehicle is about 150–300 m out or under 60 seconds away (estimate). Second, when two vehicles arrive together the app should announce their order ("22 first, 3 about 40 metres behind") and never claim to know which one is at the door.

| Approach | What it can say reliably | User effort | Hardware cost | Main dependency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPS feed + phone location + ETA | "Route 22, about 1 minute away." Weak on which vehicle is at the door | None; runs in background | ~€0 | Feed access from RTEC/PUA, Dekart or Tranzy |
| Camera route-number recognition | 82–95% in papers, on small datasets ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-99-3656-4_8); [Algorithms 2025](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390%2FA18100616)) (snippet) | High: user must aim at a moving bus | €0 | Battery; night and rain unknown |
| BLE beacon on each vehicle | Identifies a specific vehicle at ~50–60 m | Low | ~€11 per beacon ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.bg/en/products/detail/minew/MWB01/26724088)); ~€7.2k for 650+ vehicles (estimate), before installation | Operator installs and maintains |
| Hybrid (GPS ETA + beacon or camera confirmation) | Best available | Low to medium | Medium | Both of the above |

The camera route deserves a fair hearing, because it needs no partner. The best result, **95% on 120 hard images**, came from checking the OCR output against the list of routes known to serve the stop ([Algorithms 18(10):616](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390%2FA18100616)) (snippet). Super-resolution OCR reached **85.1% on a dataset of low-quality LED signs** ([Nottingham Ningbo](https://research.nottingham.edu.cn/en/publications/mamba-based-pp-ocr-enhanced-with-super-resolution-for-bus-route-n/)) (snippet), and LED signs are what Chișinău's vehicles carry. The researchers found **no field trial with blind users**, and asking someone with 2% vision to point a phone at an approaching trolleybus at the curb is a safety and usability problem. Camera recognition belongs in the app as an optional "confirm" button that uses the feed's list of expected routes. It should not be the main feature.

Voice output is not a blocker. Apple's iOS 26 availability pages list **Romanian and Russian for VoiceOver and Spoken Content** ([Apple](https://apple.com/uk/ios/feature-availability/)). On Android, Romanian depends on which speech engine is installed, and some Samsung engines lack it, though third-party options exist ([Lili Romanian TTS](https://eduardm.codeberg.page/romanian-tts-lili-android/)). The app should check for a Romanian voice on first launch and walk the user through installing Google's voice data if it is missing.

## Recommendation: a free GPS-first app, co-owned with the blind community

**Type of app.** Build a native, voice-first mobile app with a single job: tell me when my route is about to reach my stop, then tell me what is arriving now. Use React Native or native iOS and Android, but test early with VoiceOver and TalkBack. A cross-platform layer that fights the screen reader is the most common way accessibility apps fail. Do not build a general trip planner, because Moovit and Google already cover that. Make it free to users, as NaviLens GO, Moovit and Seeing AI are, and fund it through the city, EU or UNDP accessibility programmes, or telecom CSR budgets. A few thousand users will not support subscriptions (estimate).

**MVP scope (3–5 months, 2–4 people; estimate).** The MVP should:

- Find the nearest stop, and let the user save favourite stops and routes.
- Announce a chosen route about 60 seconds out and again as it arrives, in Romanian or Russian.
- Use distinct vibration patterns, so the alert works in street noise or with the phone in a pocket.
- Read out, on a single tap, every tracked vehicle coming toward this stop, in order.
- Send notifications to a smartwatch.
- Say explicitly when a route has no live data ("minibus 117: no tracking, schedule only").

Everything else waits.

**Architecture.** Run a small backend that subscribes to the Dekart MQTT topics, or polls Tranzy, behind an adapter layer so the source can be switched to a future official GTFS-Realtime feed ([GTFS-RT](https://gtfs.org/documentation/realtime/reference/)). The backend matches each vehicle to a route segment to work out its direction, using Roataway's station and segment data, and pushes alerts to subscribed phones. The phone only needs to know which stop the user is at and to speak through the system TTS. Hosting should cost tens of euros a month at this scale (estimate).

**Phases.**

| Phase | Timing | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Weeks 1–2 | Connect to the broker. Measure the share of vehicles with trackers, the latency and the heading quality at five real stops. Interview 8–10 blind riders through the association. |
| 1 | Months 1–5 | MVP as above, with a closed beta of about 20–30 users. |
| 2 | Months 6–12 | Formal data agreement. A "request stop" alert to the driver, Gyeonggi-style, through RTEC's on-board terminals or a cheap driver-side device. Optional camera confirmation. |
| 3 | Year 2 | BLE beacons on vehicles (pilot 2–3 routes first) or a NaviLens/STEP HEAR-style deployment. Lobby for external audio announcements. |

**Partners.** The essential ones are the **Asociația Nevăzătorilor din Moldova** and the Society of the Blind, to co-design the app and recruit testers; **RTEC, PUA and Primăria** (the City Hall), which own the data and could authorise driver alerts; **Dekart**, which runs the tracker feed; and **Tranzy**, the commercial API and possibly an integration partner, since it announced an audio feature and seems not to have shipped one. Worth approaching: STB Bucharest and the Asociația Nevăzătorilor din România for STEP HEAR lessons; the UNDP and EU "MOVE IT like Lublin" teams, which already work on Chișinău real-time data ([UNDP](https://undp.cz/?p=9655); [EU4Moldova](https://eu4moldova.eu/en/first-smart-traffic-sensors-installed-by-the-traffic-monitoring-center/)); and the 150+ digital stop panels the city has planned ([IPN](https://ipn.md/en/over-150-digital-information-panels-to-be-installed-at-waiting-7967_1103150.html)) (snippet), which could get audio output.

**Risks, in order:**

1. **The feed disappears.** A vendor change or a revoked licence kills the app overnight. Get a written data agreement before any public launch.
2. **False confidence.** A wrong "22 is here" can put someone on the wrong vehicle or into traffic. Use predictive, probabilistic wording, and treat untracked vehicles as unknown rather than absent.
3. **Coverage gaps,** mainly *rutiere* and vehicles without trackers.
4. **Android Romanian TTS** is missing on some phones.
5. **Abandonment.** The Warsaw and 2015 Bucharest systems faded. Find an institutional owner early.
6. **Privacy.** User location is personal data under Moldovan law. Keep it on the device where you can.

## Conclusion

The friend's instinct is right, but the value is not in the technology. Chișinău already collects the hard data and lets it go unused for the people who need it most, so the app's job is to turn an existing 3-second GPS stream into one spoken sentence at the curb. That makes the project mostly about partnerships and trust. The cheapest MVP is also the one that most needs the operator's goodwill, and the only fully reliable answer to "which vehicle is in front of me" (a beacon or an announcement on the vehicle itself) needs the operator even more. Treat the app as the first step of an advocacy campaign as well as a product.

Two unknowns decide whether this works, and both can be settled in a fortnight: whether the Dekart broker still publishes, with what share of the fleet tracked, and whether blind riders in Chișinău mainly need "tell me when it's coming", which GPS can do, or "tell me which one is at the door", which needs beacons or drivers. Answer those first at five real stops with real users. Every other decision in this report depends on them.
